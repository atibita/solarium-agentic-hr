# Welcome to Solarium

This overview introduces the full set of Solarium policy and procedure documents (the "Policy Corpus"). It explains how the documents are organized, who they apply to, how exceptions are handled, and where to go with questions. Every other document in the corpus assumes you have read this overview first.

Solarium is a distributed technology company with employees working from company offices, home offices, and client sites across multiple countries. Because our workforce is spread out, we rely on clearly written, centrally maintained policies rather than tribal knowledge or "ask around" culture. If a policy doesn't answer your question, that is treated as a documentation defect, not a signal to guess — please report it to Governance so we can fix it.

## Who These Policies Apply To

Unless a specific document says otherwise, these policies apply to:

- All full-time and part-time regular employees of Solarium Inc. and its wholly owned subsidiaries
- Fixed-term and returning seasonal employees, with adjustments noted in the relevant policy
- Interns, who follow the Intern Addendum referenced in the Onboarding Guide

Contractors, consultants, and agency staff are generally covered only by the Data Security and Acceptable Use Policy and the Workplace Conduct Policy, since those govern access to company systems and behavior on company premises or calls. Contract terms in individual statements of work take precedence over this handbook where they conflict.

## How Policies Are Governed

- **Owner**: Each document has a single accountable owner listed in its header. The owner is responsible for keeping the content accurate and for approving exceptions within their authority.
- **Review cycle**: All policies are formally reviewed at least once every 12 months, or sooner if a law, benefit vendor, or major process changes. The "Effective" date on each document reflects the most recent substantive update.
- **Version numbers**: A change to the *number before the decimal* (e.g., 3.0 → 4.0) means a material policy change that may require re-acknowledgment. A change *after* the decimal (e.g., 3.1 → 3.2) means a clarification, typo fix, or formatting update with no change in substance.
- **Approval authority**: Substantive changes to any HR or Finance policy require sign-off from the VP of People and the Controller. Security policy changes additionally require sign-off from the CISO. Governance coordinates but does not unilaterally rewrite policy content.

## Exceptions and Interpretation

Nobody expects every situation to fit neatly into a policy written for the general case. If your situation isn't clearly covered, or a strict reading of a policy would produce an obviously unreasonable result:

1. Talk to your manager first. Many "exceptions" are really just normal manager judgment calls (e.g., approving a slightly late expense report).
2. If it involves money, legal risk, or a benefit outside your manager's authority, your manager escalates to the relevant document owner (see the table above) or to Governance.
3. Governance logs all formal exceptions in the Policy Exception Register so that repeated exceptions can be turned into policy updates instead of quietly becoming "the way things really work."

Exceptions are not precedent. An exception granted to one employee does not automatically apply to others in a similar situation — ask rather than assume.

## Language, Regional Law, and Conflicts

These policies are written primarily from a U.S.-headquartered perspective. Where local law in your country or state provides a *more generous* benefit than what's written here (for example, statutory minimum leave), local law governs and Solarium will apply the more generous standard. Where local law requires something *stricter* than our default process (for example, additional data privacy consent), the stricter standard governs. People Operations maintains country-specific addenda for offices outside the United States; ask your local HR partner if you're unsure which addendum applies to you.

If two Solarium documents appear to conflict, the more specific document controls over the more general one, and the more recently dated version controls over an older one. When in doubt, ask — don't guess and don't assume the more convenient reading is correct.

## How to Ask a Question

- **General policy questions**: Post in the #ask-people-ops channel or email people-ops@solarium.example. Most questions get a same-business-day answer.
- **Security incidents or suspected data exposure**: Follow the reporting steps in SOL-SEC-401 immediately — do not wait for a "normal" response time.
- **Conduct or harassment concerns**: See the reporting channels in SOL-HR-105. You never have to go through your own manager if the concern involves them.
- **Something that seems wrong or out of date in these documents**: Email governance@solarium.example with the document ID and section. We treat these reports as a favor, not a complaint.

## Acknowledgment

New employees acknowledge receipt of the full Policy Corpus during onboarding (see SOL-HR-104). Continued employment after a material policy update constitutes acknowledgment of the updated policy, but Governance will always send a summary of what changed and why — we don't rely on employees combing through redlines.
