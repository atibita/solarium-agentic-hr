SOLARIUM INC.
Equipment and IT Asset Policy
Document ID: SOL-OPS-202 | Version 2.3 | Effective: January 1, 2026 | Owner: Director of IT Operations | Classification: Internal — All Employees

---

# 1. Purpose

This policy explains what equipment Solarium provides, how to request additional hardware or software, how to handle repairs and loss, and what's expected when equipment is returned.

# 2. Standard Equipment Package

Every new regular employee is issued the following as part of onboarding, coordinated with IT before or on their start date:

- One laptop (choice of a standard-configuration MacBook Pro 14" or a Dell/Lenovo Windows business laptop; see the current models on the IT portal)
- One monitor (27" standard; a second monitor is available on request for roles that benefit from multi-monitor setups, e.g., engineering, design, finance)
- Keyboard, mouse, and laptop stand
- Headset for calls
- Laptop bag

Remote employees receive the same package shipped to their home address; office-based employees may pick up equipment at their local office or have it shipped, at their preference.

# 3. Requesting Additional or Alternate Equipment

- **Role-based add-ons** (e.g., a graphics tablet for design roles, a second monitor, specialized peripherals) can be requested through the IT ticketing system and are typically pre-approved for common role/equipment combinations — check the "Standard Role Kits" list first, as it often skips the approval wait entirely.
- **Non-standard requests** (equipment not on any standard list, or above the standard price band) require manager approval in the ticket before IT will fulfill it.
- **Ergonomic equipment** (external keyboard, chair, footrest, monitor arm, sit-stand desk) can be requested without special justification up to $300 per employee per rolling 3-year period; requests above that amount should include a note from a healthcare provider or an ergonomic assessment, which People Operations can help arrange at no cost to you.
- Standard turnaround for approved requests is 5–10 business days depending on shipping location.

# 4. Software and Application Access

- Standard software (productivity suite, communication tools, password manager, approved AI assistant) is provisioned automatically on Day 1.
- Role-specific software (e.g., design tools, developer IDEs, analytics platforms) is requested through the IT portal and typically auto-approved for your role's standard kit.
- Requests for software outside the approved catalog require both manager approval and a Security review per SOL-SEC-401, since new software can introduce security and licensing risk. Budget for the license, if any, comes from your team's budget, not IT's general budget — check with your manager before submitting if cost is a factor.
- Do not install software that requires local administrator rights outside the approved catalog, even for a "quick test" — submit a ticket instead.

# 5. Home Office Setup

See SOL-OPS-201 (Remote and Flexible Work Policy) for the home office stipend, which is separate from the standard equipment package above and covers items like desks, chairs, and lighting that you select and purchase yourself.

# 6. Repairs, Damage, and Loss

- **Hardware malfunction**: file an IT ticket; IT will troubleshoot remotely first and arrange a loaner or replacement if the issue can't be resolved that way, typically within 2 business days for office-based employees and 3–5 business days (accounting for shipping) for remote employees.
- **Accidental damage** (e.g., spilled coffee, dropped laptop): report it promptly — there is no penalty for reporting; the equipment will simply be repaired or replaced. Repeated accidental damage to the same employee's equipment (three or more incidents in 12 months) will prompt a conversation with your manager about workspace practices, not automatic discipline.
- **Loss or theft**: report to IT Security within 1 hour of discovery per SOL-SEC-401, and file a police report if the loss occurred outside a company facility (e.g., stolen from a car or during travel) — IT will provide a letter confirming the device's replacement value if needed for the report.
- **Willful damage or gross negligence** (e.g., leaving a laptop unattended and unlocked in a clearly unsafe public location leading to theft) may result in the employee being asked to cover part of the replacement cost, determined case by case by IT and People Operations together — this is intentionally rare and always includes a conversation with the employee first, not a unilateral payroll deduction.

# 7. Asset Tracking

All issued hardware is tagged with a Solarium asset ID and tracked in the IT asset management system, tied to your employee record. You can view your currently assigned assets at any time on the IT self-service portal. Please keep this list accurate — if you're issued a loaner or temporary device, note the expected return date in the ticket.

# 8. Equipment Refresh Cycle

Laptops are eligible for refresh every 3 years, or sooner if a role genuinely requires it (e.g., a performance-sensitive engineering or design workload) — request an early refresh through IT with a brief justification. Monitors and peripherals are refreshed on an as-needed basis rather than a fixed cycle; if something no longer meets your needs, file a ticket.

# 9. Returning Equipment

- **Voluntary or involuntary termination**: return all company equipment by your last working day if local, or within 5 business days using the prepaid shipping label IT provides for remote employees.
- **Internal transfer or role change**: keep your standard equipment; return any role-specific equipment no longer needed for your new role within 30 days, coordinated with your new and former manager.
- **Leave of absence longer than 60 days**: IT may request equipment be returned or securely stored for the duration if it will otherwise sit idle and unpatched; you'll be notified in advance and equipment is reissued promptly on your return.
- Failure to return equipment after departure will result in a follow-up from People Operations and, if unresolved, may be treated as an unreturned company asset subject to the terms of your offer letter and applicable law regarding final pay deductions (which vary by state/country — Payroll will confirm what applies to you before any deduction is made).

# 10. Personal Use of Company Equipment

Reasonable personal use of your company laptop (personal email, browsing, streaming during breaks) is fine, consistent with SOL-SEC-401's acceptable use rules. Company equipment remains company property and may be remotely wiped or recovered if lost, stolen, or upon termination — back up any personal files stored on it elsewhere before that becomes relevant, since IT does not guarantee recovery of personal data from a company device.
