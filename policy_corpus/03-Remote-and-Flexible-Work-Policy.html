SOLARIUM INC.

Remote and Flexible Work Policy

Document ID: SOL-OPS-201  |  Version 3.4  |  Effective: January 1, 2026  |  Owner: VP, People Operations & Director of IT Operations  |  Classification: Internal — All Employees

1. Purpose and Work Models

Solarium supports three work models. Your work model is set when you're hired or when you change roles, and is recorded in your employee record. It's not something you choose informally week to week — see Section 6 if your needs change.

ModelDescriptionTypical roles
RemoteNo assigned office; you work from your home address (or an approved alternate location, see Section 5) on a permanent basis.Engineering, support, most individual-contributor roles
HybridAssigned to a Solarium office; expected in-office a minimum number of days set by your team (typically 2–3 per week).Roles with regular in-person collaboration needs, some management roles
Office-basedAssigned to a Solarium office; expected in-office all scheduled working days, e.g., due to equipment or physical presence requirements.Lab/hardware roles, front-desk/facilities, some client-facing roles

2. Core Collaboration Hours

Regardless of work model, all employees maintain at least 4 hours of overlap with their team's designated core hours, published by each manager and generally falling between 10:00 a.m. and 3:00 p.m. in the team's primary time zone. Outside core hours, you may structure your day flexibly — start early, take a long midday break, work late — as long as your deliverables and meeting commitments are met.

Flexibility is about when you work, not how much. Standard full-time expectation remains 40 hours/week (or your contracted part-time equivalent), tracked by outcomes and manager check-ins rather than a timesheet, except for roles legally required to track hours (see your offer letter).

3. Eligibility for Remote or Hybrid Work

Eligibility is primarily role-based, decided when a position is created, not negotiated individually after hire except through the process in Section 6. Factors that determine a role's eligible model include:

  • Whether the role requires hands-on access to physical equipment, inventory, or lab space
  • Whether the role has a legal or client-contractual requirement for on-site presence
  • Whether the team has determined in-person collaboration is materially better for the type of work (e.g., early-stage product design)

New hires must work from a country/state where Solarium is registered to employ, listed on the internal "Approved Work Locations" page — this is a legal/tax requirement, not a preference, and cannot be waived by a manager.

4. Home Office Stipend

Remote and hybrid employees receive a one-time $750 home office setup stipend within their first 60 days, plus an ongoing $50/month stipend to offset home internet and utility costs, paid via expense reimbursement (see SOL-FIN-301) or added to payroll where local tax law requires that treatment.

StipendAmountCovers
One-time setup$750Desk, chair, monitor arm, lighting, cabling — items you select and own personally

The one-time stipend does not cover computer hardware — see SOL-OPS-202 for company-issued equipment. Unused stipend does not roll over or convert to cash.

5. Working from an Alternate Location

Occasionally working from somewhere other than your registered home address (a co-working space, a family member's home, a short trip) is fine without special approval, subject to:

  • Within your home country: No approval needed for trips under 30 consecutive days; notify your manager as a courtesy.
  • International or 30+ consecutive days: Submit a "Temporary Work Location" request at least 10 business days in advance via the People Operations portal. This exists because working from a different country can trigger tax, visa, and legal obligations for Solarium, not because we don't trust you to work from a coffee shop in another city.
  • Requests outside an Approved Work Location country may be denied or restricted in duration based on legal/tax constraints outside Solarium's control — this is the most common reason a request doesn't get approved, and it isn't personal.

6. Changing Your Work Model

If your circumstances change (e.g., you want to move from hybrid to remote, or relocate), talk to your manager first. Your manager and their HR business partner will assess:

  1. Whether your specific role still requires an office-based or hybrid presence
  2. Whether the new location is an Approved Work Location
  3. Any team-level impact (e.g., if a role's core function depends on physical proximity to a lab)

Decisions are typically made within 2–3 weeks. A denial should always come with a reason — if you don't get one, escalate to your HR business partner.

7. Equipment, Security, and Workspace Expectations for Remote Work

  • Maintain a dedicated, reasonably private workspace during working hours, especially for calls involving Confidential or Restricted data (see SOL-SEC-401).
  • Follow the device and network security requirements in SOL-SEC-401, including VPN use and router security basics.
  • Camera-on is the team default for scheduled meetings unless your manager sets a different norm for their team — audio-only is always fine for large all-hands or optional sessions.

8. International Travel and Work While Traveling

Working occasionally while on personal travel (checking in, light coverage) is fine within the alternate-location rules in Section 5. If you plan to work substantively (not just check messages) while traveling internationally for more than a few days, treat it as a Temporary Work Location request. Purely personal vacation time should be logged as PTO per SOL-HR-101, not blended informally with "working from the beach" — this protects both your actual time off and Solarium's tax compliance.

9. In-Office Expectations for Hybrid Employees

Hybrid employees' minimum in-office days are set by their manager and communicated at least 2 weeks before taking effect or changing. Reasonable, occasional flexibility (e.g., swapping which days you're in for a given week) doesn't require formal approval — just coordinate with your manager and team so scheduled in-person collaboration still happens.
